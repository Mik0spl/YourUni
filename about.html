What this site knows, and what it doesn't

A tool that estimates your future is only useful if it is honest about its limits. Here are ours.

Where the numbers come from

Every figure is compiled by hand from public sources: university admissions pages, published fee schedules, national statistics offices and the major ranking bodies. They are approximations, and they age.

  • ✱Rankings are a composite position across the major world rankings rather than any single table. Different rankings disagree substantially — treat a difference of twenty places as noise.
  • ✱Acceptance rates are the most recent published overall rate. Where a university publishes a separate international rate, we store both, and the chance model uses the international one. Rates for a specific course are often far lower than the university-wide figure — computer science almost everywhere is the clearest example.
  • ✱Tuition and living costs are annual, in local currency, for an international undergraduate. Living costs are a mid-range estimate for that city, not a frugal one. Cross-border comparisons convert at indicative rates you can update in data/reference/currencies.js.
  • ✱Entry requirements are the typical published offer, not the minimum a university has ever accepted.
  • ✱Quality-of-life scores are our own composite of six measures for the city, weighted towards what matters to a student: safety, affordability, getting around, culture, nature access and student life.
⚠ Always confirm fees, deadlines and requirements on the university's own website before you rely on them. Nothing here is an offer, and nothing here is advice.

How admission chances are calculated

The estimate starts from the university's published acceptance rate and moves it based on how you compare to what they ask for. In log-odds terms:

  1. Your grades against theirs. Every grading system is converted onto one shared 0–100 academic index. The distance between your index and the university's requirement is the single biggest input, and falling short costs more than exceeding it gains.
  2. Test scores, but only where the university actually reads them. UC Berkeley does not consider the SAT at all, so a 1600 changes nothing there.
  3. Activities, weighted by country. A US committee reads your whole application; a German numerus clausus place is allocated by Abitur grade. The same activity record is multiplied by a per-country weight between 0.1 and 1.0.
  4. Subject fit. Applying to one of a university's strongest departments helps a little; applying for something they barely teach hurts.
  5. English proficiency as a gate. Falling below the stated minimum is heavily penalised because those applications are usually rejected before they are read. Exceeding it adds nothing.

"What would help most" is not a guess either — the model is re-run with each change applied and reports the actual difference in the resulting probability.

What it cannot see

Your essays, your references, your interview, whether the university needs a bassoonist this year, or whether a department quietly halved its intake. Treat the number as a way to sort a hundred universities into "realistic" and "not" — which it does well — rather than a prediction about you specifically.

Privacy

There is no account, no login and no server that stores anything about you.

  • ✓Your grades, subjects, activities, shortlist and comparison are written to localStorage in your own browser under the key youruni.profile.v1.
  • ✓Nothing is transmitted anywhere. The site is static files — there is no backend to send data to.
  • ✓No analytics, no cookies, no third-party trackers. The only external request the site makes is to Google Fonts for its two typefaces.
  • ✓Erase everything on the profile page clears it permanently, as does clearing your browser's site data.

This matters more than usual here because the people using this site are often under 18. The simplest way to protect that data is not to collect it.

Accessibility and design

Charts never use colour alone — every admission band carries an icon and a written label, cost breakdowns ship a table view, and the categorical palette is validated for protanopia and deuteranopia separation rather than picked by eye. The site works in light and dark, follows your system setting by default, and respects reduced-motion.

Adding or correcting data

All data lives in plain JavaScript files under /data, split by region so they stay editable. Adding a university means appending an object to a regional file; adding a whole grading system means appending its conversion anchors. Run npm run validate and it will tell you what is wrong before anything reaches the page.

Found something out of date? The fix belongs in the data file, not the code.
